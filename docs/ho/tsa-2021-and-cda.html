TSA 2021 and CDA

The Telecommunications (Security) Act 2021 in brief, and what it means for a retention and disclosure capability

Prepared for Michael Rossi · October 2026 — legal brief, not legal advice

In one paragraph

The Telecommunications (Security) Act 2021 amended the Communications Act 2003 to place a statutory duty on providers of public electronic communications networks and services to identify and reduce the risk of security compromises, and to prepare for and remediate them. The detail sits in the Electronic Communications (Security Measures) Regulations 2022 and a Code of Practice, tiered by provider size, with Ofcom as the regulator and fines of up to 10% of relevant turnover. For CDA the point is simple: the retention and disclosure estate is part of the network. It is inside the TSA perimeter, assessed by Ofcom to TSA standards, and cannot be run to lawful-intercept-team norms in isolation from enterprise security governance.

TALKING POINT

'The CDA platform is a network asset under TSA. Ofcom will look at it with the same lens as the core, and the IPA's own 'same standard as business data' test now has a statutory floor to point at.'

1. What the Act does

ProvisionSubstance
s.105A Communications Act (inserted)Duty to take appropriate and proportionate measures to identify and reduce the risks of security compromises, and to prepare for their occurrence
s.105B–CDuty to take measures in response to a compromise; power for the Secretary of State to make Security Measures Regulations
s.105E–FCodes of Practice: Ofcom must take them into account; non-compliance with a Code is evidence of breach of duty
s.105J–KDuty to inform users and Ofcom of compromises with significant effect
s.105M–POfcom's enforcement: information powers, assessments, directions, penalties up to 10% of relevant turnover or £100,000 per day
s.105Z1 onwardDesignated vendor directions — the Secretary of State may restrict use of named high-risk vendors (the Huawei provisions)
Security Measures Regulations 2022The 'what': network architecture, protection of data and functions, monitoring and analysis, supply chain, privileged access, patching, testing, governance, competency
Code of Practice (2022)The 'how' and 'by when': Tier 1 (large, >£1bn turnover) by March 2024 / 2025 / 2027 for successive tranches; Tier 2 (>£50m) a year later; Tier 3 proportionate

'Security compromise' is defined broadly: anything that compromises availability, performance or functionality of the network, unauthorised access to or interference with it, or anything that compromises the confidentiality of signals or data. A wrongful disclosure from a retained-data store is a compromise of the confidentiality of data conveyed by the network — it is in scope.

4. Difficulties in practice

  • Perimeter blindness. LI and retention estates have historically been run by a small, cleared team outside mainstream IT governance. TSA assessments will ask for them to be in the asset inventory, the risk register and the testing programme like any other security-critical function.
  • Tier timing. Tier 1 operators' deadlines have passed for the first tranches; a CDA programme cannot claim a longer runway than the network it sits in.
  • Log retention exceeds data retention. Thirteen months of security logs for a store whose data lives twelve: the audit will outlive the data it describes, which is what the CDA course wants anyway, but it must be designed, not assumed.
  • Supply chain. Retention and mediation platforms are often a single specialist vendor with deep access. TSA makes the 'we trust them' answer unacceptable in writing.
  • Conflicting incident clocks. ICO 72 hours, Ofcom 'without undue delay' for significant compromises, IPCO per the Code. Decide the routing before the incident.
  • Designated vendors. If any component in the CDA path is from a vendor subject to a designated vendor direction, the removal timetable applies to it.

5. Questions to ask

  • Is the retention and disclosure estate in the TSA asset inventory as a security-critical function, and in which tier's timetable?
  • Who in the TSA governance structure is accountable for it, and is that the same person who owns the IPA capability?
  • Are disclosure audit logs retained for at least thirteen months on a lifecycle separate from the retained data?
  • Show me the vendor access model and the contract clauses that back it.
  • Which incident routes — Ofcom, ICO, IPCO — are pre-agreed for a compromise of the retained store, and who decides on the day?
  • Has the disclosure interface been penetration tested, and is the report in the assurance pack?

CAVEAT

Tier thresholds, deadlines and the Code's detailed measures are as understood at October 2026 and should be checked against the current Regulations, Code and Ofcom guidance before use as a design constraint.

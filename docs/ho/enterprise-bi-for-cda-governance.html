Who this is for and how to use it

This guide assumes you have designed and governed large systems but have not run a BI or data-warehouse function yourself. It teaches the practices from the ground up — enough to understand what a BI professional means, to recognise the same practice when it appears in a CDA design under a different name, and to hold a credible conversation with either side. Each chapter has the same shape: what the practice is, the vocabulary, how it shows up in CDA, what you can say about it from an architect's standpoint, and what you should not claim. The honest position to take throughout: I have governed the systems these practices run inside, I understand them as an architect understands them, and I have not personally built a warehouse. That is a strong position. The disciplines below are the ones a CDA design authority has to hold people to; the person holding them to account does not need to have written the ETL.

1. The data platform and its layers

What it is

Enterprise BI is the discipline of taking data out of the systems that run the business (operational or transactional systems — the CRM, the billing platform, the order system) and putting it somewhere it can be queried for understanding rather than for operation. The 'somewhere' has had several names over thirty years — data warehouse, data mart, data lake, lakehouse — but the structure is constant.

TermMeaning
Operational / transactional system (OLTP)A system that runs the business and is optimised for many small writes. The source. In CDA: the IMS nodes, HSS, PGW, NAT, CRM
Data warehouseA store optimised for querying large volumes of historical data, structured into a model. Kimball-style (star schemas) or Inmon-style (normalised then marts). In CDA: the retention store

Data martA subject-specific slice of the warehouse for one consumer group

Semantic layerThe layer that defines business terms over physical tables — 'revenue' means these columns combined this way — and applies access rules. In CDA: disclosure management, where scope and caveats are applied

What you can say

'A CDA disclosure capability is a data platform with a legally defined consumer. The layers are the ones any warehouse architect would draw — sources, staging, store, a semantic layer that applies the rules, a serving interface — and the controls sit on the boundaries between them. What is different is that the semantic layer's rules come from an authorisation rather than a business definition.'

What not to claim

Do not claim to have chosen between Kimball and Inmon, tuned a warehouse, or built a lakehouse. Say you have governed platforms that had one, and that the layer boundaries are where you have always put the controls.

2. Data modelling: facts, dimensions and keys

What it is

Warehouse data is modelled as facts — things that happened, with measures (a sale, a call, a login) — and dimensions — the things facts are about (customer, product, date, location). A star schema is one fact table surrounded by dimension tables, joined on keys. The fact table is large and append-only; dimensions are smaller and describe.

                 | dim_date   |
                 +-----+------+
                       |
 +-------------+   +---+----------------+   +----------------+
 | dim_customer|---| fact_call          |---| dim_cell_site  |

 +-------------+   | cell_key (FK)      |
 | dim_device  |---| date_key (FK)      |
 +-------------+   | duration, outcome  |
                   +--------------------+

FactAn event or transaction with measuresEvents data: a session, a registration, a message
DimensionDescriptive context a fact refers toEntity data: the subscriber, the device, the cell site
GrainWhat one row of a fact table represents — one call, one call leg, one bearer eventThe design authority's decision about what one retained record is; the ICID correlation question is a grain question
Business key / natural keyThe identifier the source uses (MSISDN, account number)MSISDN, IMSI, IMPU — reusable, so not safe as a sole key
Surrogate keyA warehouse-generated key that is stable even when the business key is reassignedAn internal subscription identifier that survives number recycling

What you can say

'The entity/events split in the Act is the dimension/fact split in a warehouse, with a legal threshold attached. The thing a modeller would ask first — what is the grain of a retained record? — is the question that decides whether one call is one row or six, and therefore whether correlation is done at ingest or at query time.'

What not to claim

Do not claim to have designed star schemas. You can say you have reviewed data models for large systems and that grain and key stability are the two things you always check.

3. Slowly changing dimensions and point-in-time correctness

What it is

Dimensions change: a customer moves, a product is renamed, a number is reassigned. A warehouse has to decide whether to overwrite (and lose history) or keep history. The standard answer is the Type 2 slowly changing dimension: each version of a dimension row has a valid-from and valid-to date, and a fact joins to the version that was in force when the fact happened.

Type 1 (overwrite)            Type 2 (versioned)
msisdn   customer             msisdn   customer   valid_from   valid_to     current
0771...  B                    0771...  A          2025-06-01   2026-02-14   N
                              0771...  (none)     2026-02-15   2026-02-19   N
                              0771...  B          2026-02-20   9999-12-31   Y

Type 1 answers 'who has this number?'  Type 2 answers 'who had it on 10 Feb?'

The classic failure is a Type 1 join against historical facts: every call ever made on the number is now attributed to customer B, including the ones customer A made. In a sales warehouse that misstates a region's revenue. In CDA it attributes someone else's communications to the wrong person.

TermMeaning
SCD Type 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 6Never change / overwrite / version with dates / keep previous value in a column / hybrid
Effective dating, bitemporalValid time (when it was true in the world) versus transaction time (when the system learned it). Bitemporal stores both — relevant when a binding is corrected after the fact
Point-in-time (PIT) query / 'as-at'A query that joins facts to the dimension version in force at the fact's time
Late-arriving dimensionA fact arrives before its dimension row exists — the subscriber is not yet in the reference data
GapA period with no valid version — the number was unallocated

What you can say

'Every identifier lookup is only valid with respect to a time — that is a Type 2 dimension with explicit gaps. The question I ask of any resolution design is whether it is bitemporal: does it record when the binding was true and when we learned it, so that a correction after the fact is auditable rather than silent?'

What not to claim

Do not claim to have implemented SCDs. Say you have seen what happens when a system joins current reference data to historical records, and that you treat as-at correctness as a non-negotiable requirement.

4. Master data management and identity resolution

What it is

Master data management (MDM) is the practice of maintaining one authoritative version of the core entities an organisation cares about — customer, product, supplier, location — when those entities appear, inconsistently, in many systems. The output is a golden record per entity with a cross-reference to each source system's key. The hard part is entity resolution: deciding that 'M. Rossi, 12 High St' in the CRM and 'Michael Rossi, 12 High Street' in billing are the same person.

TermMeaningCDA counterpart
Golden recordThe single trusted version of an entityThe subscription record in HSS/UDM plus the CRM account
Cross-reference (xref)The table mapping each source key to the golden recordThe binding store: MSISDN, IMSI, IMEI, IMPU, account — each with validity
Deterministic matchingKeys match exactly by ruleThe only kind allowed in the CDA resolution path
Probabilistic / fuzzy matchingA score from similarity of name, address, date of birth; above a threshold, mergeRuled out: nobody can explain a score in a witness box
Survivorship rulesWhich source wins when they disagreeSystem-of-record nomination per attribute
Hierarchy / relationship managementHouseholds, corporate familiesIdentifier-to-identifier hops — each one a potential disclosure about a different person
StewardshipHumans resolving the cases rules cannotThe human review point; the 'insufficient information' nil return

What you can say

'Identity resolution in CDA is an MDM cross-reference with three constraints MDM does not usually have: every binding is time-qualified, matching must be deterministic and explicable, and every hop from one identifier to another is a disclosure in its own right that needs its own authority. If a vendor's resolution engine has a match score in it, it is in the wrong place.'

What not to claim

Do not claim to have run an MDM programme. You can say you have governed systems where the same party appeared under several keys and that you know where the probabilistic shortcuts tend to be hidden.

5. ETL/ELT and the pipeline

What it is

ETL — extract, transform, load — is the pipeline that moves data from sources to the store. ELT loads first and transforms inside the store, which is the modern default on cloud platforms. Pipelines are batch (nightly extracts) or streaming (events as they happen) and are run by an orchestrator that schedules jobs, handles dependencies and retries.

TermMeaningCDA counterpart
Extract / connector / CDCReading from the source; change data capture reads the source's own change log rather than querying itFeeds from IMS nodes, NAT, HSS — some pushed by the node, some pulled
TransformParsing, cleansing, canonicalising, deriving, joiningNormalisation, truncation of content-bearing fields, classification, correlation on ICID
LoadWriting to the store, typically into partitionsWriting to time-partitioned, classified, immutable partitions
IdempotencyRe-running a job does not double-loadA replayed feed must not create duplicate retained records
Backfill / replayLoading historical data after a gap or a logic changeRe-ingesting after a feed outage — with the audit showing the replay
Schema drift / schema evolutionThe source changes shape; the pipeline must noticeA node upgrade adds or renames a field — the capability-impact question
Quarantine / dead-letter queueRecords that fail validation are set aside, not dropped silentlyRejected records with reasons; the completeness statement counts them
OrchestratorAirflow, Azure Data Factory, dbt Cloud, DagsterWhatever schedules and monitors the ingest jobs
StreamingKafka, Event Hubs, Kinesis; process records as they arriveNear-real-time ingest for the urgent path

What you can say

'The ingest pipeline is where the irreversible decisions happen: what gets truncated, what gets classified, what gets rejected. In a warehouse you can usually reload from source if you got the transform wrong. Here the source has deleted its copy on PECR grounds, so the pipeline has to be right first time and its rejects have to be counted, not discarded.'

What not to claim

Do not claim to have written pipelines in a particular tool. You can speak about integration platforms you have designed — message flows between enterprise systems, reconciliation, idempotency, replay — which is the same discipline.

6. Data quality

What it is

Data quality management is the practice of defining what 'good' means for a dataset, measuring it continuously, and acting on failures. The DAMA DMBOK dimensions are the common vocabulary: completeness, accuracy, timeliness, consistency, validity, uniqueness. Quality is enforced by rules (checks run in the pipeline), reconciliation (comparing counts and totals between source and target), and monitoring (dashboards and alerts on the rules).

PracticeWhat it looks like in BIIn CDA
Source-to-target reconciliationRow counts and control totals from the extract compared to what loaded; a nightly report; a threshold that pages someoneVolume reconciliation against source-side counts per feed per period — the course's single most important operational control
Data quality rulesNot-null, referential integrity, domain values, ranges; implemented in Great Expectations, dbt tests, Soda, or the platform's own frameworkIdentifier format validation, mandatory field checks, classification completeness, correlation success rate
Freshness / SLA monitoring'The sales table is 6 hours stale' alertsGeneration-to-availability latency reported at the tail (p99), not the mean
Anomaly detection on volumesToday's rows versus the expected range for this weekdayThe feed that silently stopped; the IoT population that doubled
Data quality scorecardsPer dataset, per dimension, trendedThe assurance pack's quality section
Issue managementA data quality issue log with owner and remediationThe error taxonomy with detection, notification and enumeration of affected disclosures

TALKING POINT

The one sentence to remember: measure against independent expectation, never against the feed's own count. A feed that reports 'I sent 0 records' agrees perfectly with a store that received 0. Expectation comes from history, from the source node's own counters, or from a parallel path.

What you can say

'Every BI platform I have seen fail did so quietly: a feed stopped, the counts matched because both sides were zero, and nobody noticed until the month-end report looked wrong. In CDA there is no month-end report. The reconciliation has to be designed in from day one and it has to compare against something the pipeline itself does not produce.'

7. The data catalogue and classification

What it is

A data catalogue is the inventory of what data exists, what it means, where it came from, who owns it, how sensitive it is and who may use it. Modern catalogues (Collibra, Alation, Microsoft Purview, Atlan, open-source DataHub) crawl the platform, attach business definitions, and record classification tags such as PII, confidential, financial. Classification drives access policy — ideally automatically, often only on paper.

TermMeaningCDA counterpart
Business glossaryAgreed definitions of business termsThe interpretation record: what 'data identifying the sender' means in this operator
Technical metadataTables, columns, types, sourcesThe field-level catalogue
Classification / sensitivity tagsPII, special category, confidentialEntity / events / content; ICR flag; third-party-data flag
Data owner / stewardAccountable person / day-to-day custodian per datasetDesign authority sign-off per arguable classification
Policy-as-code / tag-based accessAccess rules evaluated from the tags at query timeClassification-driven scope enforcement at the gateway
Catalogue driftThe catalogue says one thing, the schema anotherThe classification spreadsheet no code references — the course's 'compliance by documentation'

What you can say

'The test of a catalogue is whether anything reads it. If the pipeline and the access layer consume the classification, it is a control; if only the governance team does, it is a document. In CDA the classification has legal effect, so it has to be the former.'

8. Lineage and provenance

What it is

Lineage records how data moved and changed from source to output: which source tables, which transformations, which job runs, which version of the logic. Table-level lineage is common; column-level lineage is harder and more valuable. It is used for impact analysis (what breaks if this source changes?), for debugging (why is this number wrong?), and for audit (prove where this figure came from).

TermMeaningCDA counterpart
Upstream / downstreamSources feeding a dataset / consumers of itImpact analysis for the capability-impact question
Column-level lineageThis output column derives from these input columns via this logicThe provenance statement per field
Run / job lineageWhich execution produced this dataPipeline version and ingest time on every record
ReproducibilityRe-running the same logic on the same inputs gives the same outputRepeatability as a component of evidential weight
OpenLineage, MarquezOpen standards and tools for capturing lineage

What you can say

'Provenance for a court is column-level lineage with a run identifier: which node, which feed, which pipeline version, when. If the platform cannot answer that for a figure on a dashboard, it cannot answer it for a disclosure, and the evidential case rests on assertion.'

9. Access control in data platforms

What it is

Data platforms control access at several levels: to the platform, to a dataset, to rows within it (row-level security: a regional manager sees only their region), and to columns (column-level security or dynamic data masking: analysts see a hashed national insurance number). Policies are role-based (RBAC) or attribute-based (ABAC: rules on user attributes and data tags evaluated at query time).

TermMeaningCDA counterpart
RBACPermissions by roleInsufficient alone: a valid role with no live authorisation must be able to do nothing
ABAC / policy-based accessRules evaluated per request from user, resource and context attributesScope enforcement: authorisation type, data classes, period and subject intersected with the request, per query
Row-level securityFilter rows by policyPeriod and subject restriction
Column-level security / maskingHide or transform columns by policyField-set restriction by classification; truncation
Separation of dutiesNobody both builds and approves; admins cannot read dataCompartmentation of provisioning, data, disclosure and audit
Break-glassEmergency elevated access, logged and reviewedVendor and urgent-path access
Query auditEvery query logged with user, time, text, rows returnedThe requested / authorised / returned triple per disclosure

What you can say

'Role-based access tells you who may use the system; it does not tell you whether this query is lawful. That needs the authorisation as an input to the policy decision — attribute-based control evaluated per request. Most platforms can do it; few programmes configure it that way, because it is easier to trust the operator.'

10. Retention, archival and deletion

What it is

Every regulated data platform has a records retention schedule (how long each class of data is kept and why), tiered storage (hot, warm, cold, archive), and a deletion process. Since 2018 the right to erasure has forced platforms to be able to find and delete an individual's data, including in derived datasets and backups — which most could not do, and many still cannot.

TermMeaningCDA counterpart
Retention schedulePer-class retention period and legal basisThe notice period per data family
Partition pruning / lifecycle policyDrop whole partitions when they age outTime partitioning as the deletion mechanism
Legal holdSuspend deletion for specified data under litigation or investigationA designed exception with its own audit
Soft delete / tombstoneMark as deleted but keep the rowNot acceptable as deletion of retained data
Crypto-shreddingEncrypt per partition or per subject; destroy the key to deleteThe answer to backups outliving the retention period
Derived dataAggregates, extracts, ML features built from the sourceCorrelated copies, indexes, analytics extracts — all are copies for deletion purposes
Deletion evidence / certificateProof that deletion happenedRetained longer than the data it describes

What you can say

'The question I ask every platform is: when you delete, what happens in the backups? The answer is usually a pause. The only robust answer is that the backups never held readable data in the first place — per-partition keys, destroyed on expiry.'

11. Data governance as an organisational function

What it is

Data governance is the people-and-process layer: who is accountable for which data, how decisions about it are taken and recorded, how policy is set and enforced. Typical structures: a data governance council or board, domain data owners, data stewards, a data protection officer, and an architecture review board. Frameworks: DAMA DMBOK, DCAM, and in government the Data Ethics Framework and the Data Maturity Assessment.

RoleBI meaningCDA counterpart
Data ownerAccountable executive for a data domainThe controller-level accountable owner for the retained store
Data stewardDay-to-day custodian: definitions, quality, access requestsThe person who maintains the field catalogue and the interpretation record
Data governance councilCross-functional decision body for data policyThe design authority with a standing decision list
Architecture review boardReviews and approves designs against standardsThe design authority in its review function
DPOStatutory data protection adviserConsulted on the DPIA; part of the governance
Data policy / standardsDocumented rules for classification, retention, quality, accessThe negative-decision register, classification rules, nil-return vocabulary
Decision log / ADRsArchitecture decision records: context, decision, consequences, dateThe decision list the inspector will ask for

What you can say

'Governance that only reviews documents is a reading group. The design authority I run keeps a decision list — classification of arguable fields, truncation point, scope mechanism, hop bound, urgent-path scope — with the rationale and date against each, and the test is whether any of them can be produced two years later.'

This is the chapter where your actual experience is strongest; architecture governance on large programmes is the same practice. Say so.

12. Platform operations and capacity

What it is

Running a data platform means monitoring pipelines and query performance, managing storage growth, controlling cloud cost, and handling incidents. DataOps borrows DevOps practice: version-controlled transformations, automated tests, CI/CD for pipelines, observability.

PracticeMeaningCDA counterpart
Observability / data observabilityMonitoring freshness, volume, schema, distribution and lineage automatically (Monte Carlo, Bigeye, open-source Elementary)Feed monitoring; expected-versus-actual volumes; schema-drift detection
Capacity planningForecasting storage and compute from growth in sources, consumers and retentionNAT translation records dominate; indexes and replicas; steady state at twelve months
Cost management (FinOps)Attributing and controlling cloud spendCost-recovery claims under the IPA depend on being able to attribute cost to the capability
CI/CD for dataPipelines and models tested and deployed through automationChange control with the capability-impact question in the pipeline
Environment separationDev / test / prod with synthetic or masked data in non-prodTest data must never be real retained data; rehearsal of the assurance pack on synthetic data
Incident managementRunbooks, on-call, post-incident reviewThe error taxonomy; three-regulator notification routing

What you can say

'The capacity question nobody asks is the ratio of the biggest low-value dataset to the one everyone sized for. In a web platform it is the clickstream; in CDA it is NAT translation logs. I ask for that ratio, and whether it was measured or assumed.'

14. Conversation scripts

Short exchanges for the situations where the BI parallel is likely to come up. Each gives the prompt you might hear, a response that is accurate about your experience, and the follow-up question that moves the conversation onto ground you control.

You hearYou sayThen ask
'Have you worked with data warehouses before?''I have governed the platforms they sat in and reviewed the models and pipelines, rather than built them. What I bring is the control view: where the boundaries are and what has to be enforced at each.''Where in this design is scope enforced — in the application or in a service beneath it?'
'This is just a BI problem.''The disciplines are the same — catalogue, master data, reconciliation, lineage, access control. The consequences are not: the requirements come from a legal instrument, and over-delivery is a breach. That changes what we optimise for.''Which of the retained fields were chosen because the feed offered them rather than because the notice named them?'
'Our MDM engine will handle identity resolution.''Good — is its matching deterministic? Probabilistic matching is standard in MDM and it is ruled out here because a match score cannot be explained in a witness box.''Can you show me the resolution for a number that was reassigned mid-period, with the gap?'
'We have comprehensive logging.''Logging is the raw material; what I need is a reconstructable record per disclosure of what was requested, what was authorised and what was returned — and it has to outlive the data.''For a disclosure from six months ago, show me the three field sets.'
'The data quality framework covers it.''Which dimension does it measure completeness against? If it is the feed's own count, a stopped feed scores 100%.''What was yesterday's expected versus actual volume for the NAT feed?'
'Deletion is handled by the lifecycle policy.''For the live store, yes. What about the replicas, the correlated copy, the indexes and the backups?''When a partition expires, is its key destroyed?'
'We'll use the charging records; they're already consolidated.''As a first release, fine. They reflect what is billable, they change with the tariff, and they omit unanswered calls. I want a field-by-field gap analysis against the notice and a named consultee on billing changes.''Who is told before a tariff change goes live, and is the capability-impact question on the change record?'
'The vendor platform does all of this out of the box.''Then the policy — classification, scope rules, correlation, nil-return vocabulary — lives inside their product. I want it in configuration we own and can move.''If we changed supplier, what would we have to rebuild?'

TALKING POINT

The pattern in every script: acknowledge the parallel, state the difference in one sentence, and ask a question that can only be answered by showing you something. You do not need to have built the warehouse to ask to see the reconciliation report.
